Postprocess (runs on the scalar model output)

Listed in the transform table in §4.20. Fifteen distinct forms, of which identity, anti_log_linear and cox_to_years cover the large majority.

What FALCONAge implements of this

Seven per-clock preprocess ops, one dataset-level op and 21 postprocess transforms, in models/ops.py; 35 of the 69 bundled clocks declare at least one. Several are more general than their row above: scale takes centre and spread vectors stored with the model and so covers scale and scale_mean; quantile_normalize takes the gold vector and the scoring index, covering the DunedinPACE and Stubbs rows. scale_row needs statistics from the sample itself, over every probe the array carries, so it runs on the dataset before alignment.

Reference filling is not an op here. fill_nan_with_reference is the largest row in the table and FALCONAge does it in align() instead, before any op runs, because the imputation choice is a property of the dataset rather than of the clock and has to be recorded in the manifest either way. Zero is never a fill value: in β space zero means completely unmethylated, which is a real and extreme measurement, and a clock with a large positive coefficient on a probe the array does not carry will read “totally unmethylated” and shift the prediction by whole years.

Not ops, because the statistic is the whole model: mean_over_present and quantile95_over_present are AggregationClock, and the epiTOC2 / epiTOC3 row of the special passes below is DivisionClock, which averages over the sites present, as the published implementation does, where the table has nan_to_zero. The deconvolution row is DeconvolutionClock, by the authors’ non-negative least squares rather than the pseudo-inverse and simplex projection the row describes. Unbuilt: tpm_norm_log1p, autosomal_zscore, expand_with_reference, and the other eight special forward passes. Each belongs to a clock family whose coefficients are licensed, not yet traced to a primary source, or absent from the registry, so building the op first would produce code with no real answer to test against, see the tier table in Architecture.
