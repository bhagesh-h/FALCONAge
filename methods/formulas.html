Published formulas and constants

5. Exact published formulas and constants

5.1 PhenoAge (Levine 2018), clinical-biomarker version

The paper prints the model to four decimals in Table 1 and Supplementary Table S1, with γ and the inversion to years in Supplement 1. BioAge carries the same fit at full precision. Both are in the paper’s units: SI, except CRP, which enters as ln(CRP) with CRP in mg/dL.

As printed (Levine 2018 Table 1 and Supplement 1), FALCONAge’s default:

xb = −19.9067
     − 0.0336 · albumin          (g/L)
     + 0.0095 · creatinine       (µmol/L)
     + 0.1953 · glucose          (mmol/L)
     + 0.0954 · ln(CRP)          (CRP in mg/dL)
     − 0.0120 · lymphocyte%      (%)
     + 0.0268 · MCV              (fL)
     + 0.3306 · RDW              (%)
     + 0.0019 · ALP              (U/L)
     + 0.0554 · WBC              (10³/µL)
     + 0.0804 · age              (years)

γ        = 0.0076927
m        = 1 − exp( −exp(xb) · (exp(120γ) − 1) / γ )
PhenoAge = 141.50225 + ln( −0.00553 · ln(1 − m) ) / 0.090165

Full precision (BioAge phenoage0, BioAge phenoage_calc.R), coefficients="bioage":

xb = −19.90667
     − 0.03359355 · albumin      (g/L)
     + 0.009506491 · creatinine  (µmol/L)
     + 0.1953192  · glucose      (mmol/L)
     + 0.09536762 · lncrp        (BioAge: lncrp = ln(1 + CRP), CRP in mg/dL)
     − 0.01199984 · lymphocyte%  (%)
     + 0.02676401 · MCV          (fL)
     + 0.3306156  · RDW          (%)
     + 0.001868778 · ALP         (U/L)
     + 0.05542406 · WBC          (10³/µL)
     + 0.08035356 · age          (years)

m        = 1 − exp( (−1.51714 · exp(xb)) / 0.007692696 )
PhenoAge = 141.50225 + ln( −0.0055305 · ln(1 − m) ) / 0.090165

Every full-precision weight rounds to its Table 1 value, so these are the fit the table was printed from. On BioAge’s 8,924 NHANES III rows the printed table puts PhenoAge a mean 0.074 years above the full-precision fit (at most 0.14 years). With coefficients="bioage" and crp_transform="log1p" FALCONAge reproduces phenoage0 on those rows to below 0.00001 years.

Four transcription errors circulate, and all four appear in the equation printed in Liu et al. 2018 (PLoS Med 15:e1002718): alkaline phosphatase 0.00188 (Table 1: 0.0019), glucose 0.0195 (0.1953), RDW 0.3356 (0.3306), and the divisor 0.09165 (Supplement 1: 0.090165). FALCONAge carried 0.00188 until this was checked. The divisor is the costly one: BioAge corrected it in its April 2026 commit (“Update PhenoAge constant to 0.090165 and recompute data”), and code still using 0.09165 shifts results by several years. Guard m away from 0 and 1 or ln(1 − m) blows up.

The units are part of the formula. The weights are per SI unit. An implementation that applies them to albumin in g/dL, creatinine and glucose in mg/dL and CRP in mg/L without converting, as mcp-phenoage-clock does (phenoage.ts), returns nonsense: for the healthy 50-year-old of Appendix F it gives xb = 8.36 and a ten-year mortality of 1, where the converted inputs give xb = −9.07 and a PhenoAge of 41.85 years.

The CRP term is where BioAge departs from the paper. Levine 2018 enters ln(CRP). BioAge’s lncrp, the variable its NHANES PhenoAge (phenoage0) is computed from, is ln(1 + CRP) (data-raw/nhanes_all.R, lncrp = log(1 + crp)). The difference is not small. On NHANES III (n = 8,924) FALCONAge with ln(CRP) sits a mean 1.50 years below phenoage0 (SD 0.45), and not by a constant: ln(1 + CRP) − ln(CRP) = ln(1 + 1/CRP), which is largest at low CRP. With ln(1 + CRP) the gap falls to the 0.074 years of coefficient rounding, and with coefficients="bioage" as well it closes. FALCONAge follows the paper, records BioAge’s choice as a known discrepancy, and offers phenoage(df, crp_transform="log1p", coefficients="bioage") for comparison with BioAge output. Every PhenoAge computed through BioAge carries the shift.

DNAm PhenoAge is the methylation surrogate of this quantity: 513 CpGs, elastic net trained to predict the clinical PhenoAge. pyaging exposes both, phenoage (clinical, blood chemistry) and dnamphenoage (methylation).

5.2 pyaging generalised PhenoAge postprocess

The torch implementation with λ = 0.0192 (pyaging’s PhenoAge.postprocess in src/pyaging/models/_models.py, commit e7267e9):

mortality = 1 − exp( −exp(x) · (exp(120λ) − 1) / λ )
age       = 141.50225 + ln( −0.00553 · ln(1 − mortality) ) / 0.090165

5.3 BiT age (C. elegans) second correction

The published biological ages are rescaled to account for truncation of the lifespan distribution. With median lifespan 372 h (15.5 d) and SD 192/3 h (8/3 d) (AgingClock biological_age_correction.py):

corrected(y) = y − erfinv( 0.5 − ( ( erf(((372 − y)/sd)/√2)/2 ) + 0.5 ) / 2 ) · √2 · 2 · sd

Lifespan fold-change with error propagation uses uncertainties.ufloat with a 5% lifespan-assay bias and 0.5 d age-reporting bias; biological age is rescaled as chronological_age × 15.5 / reported_median_lifespan.

5.4 Zhang 2019 predictors

514 elastic-net probes or 319,607 BLUP probes, trained on 13,566 samples. Per-sample z-scoring of probes is mandatory (§4.1). Missing values are filled with the per-probe mean across individuals; probes that are NA across all individuals are dropped (DNAm-based-age-predictor pred.R). BLUP degrades more gracefully with missing probes than EN. RMSE reported as 2.04 y, the most accurate chronological clock, and the one whose acceleration stopped predicting mortality.

FALCONAge standardises over every probe the data carries, exactly as pred.R does, before the weights are applied, and leaves an absent model probe at 0 (the sample’s own mean) as pred.R does by restricting the sum to the probes present. It refuses when the data carries less than min_coverage of the 319,607 probes the models were fitted on, because a subset’s per-sample mean and SD are not the array’s. On the authors’ example (10 samples, all 485,512 probes of the 450K) it matches pred.R to 1e-13 years for the elastic net and 5e-12 for BLUP.

The scope of that standardisation is the part implementations disagree on. pyaging (commit e7267e9) takes the row mean and SD over the model’s own inputs, 514 probes for the elastic net. On the same 10 samples that places the elastic-net estimate 9.9 to 22.7 years below pred.R (mean 17.7), and its mean absolute error against chronological age rises from 4.7 to 21.3 years. The BLUP estimate moves by 0.6 to 2.0 years, because its model already spans 319,607 of the 485,512 probes.

Appendix F - Reproducing the four reference computations by hand

Small worked checks to use as smoke tests while building.

PhenoAge, from conventional units. Inputs: age 50, albumin 4.5 g/dL, creatinine 0.9 mg/dL, glucose 90 mg/dL, CRP 1.0 mg/L, lymphocyte 30%, MCV 90 fL, RDW 13%, ALP 70 U/L, WBC 6.0 ×10³/µL. Convert first, to the units of Table 1: albumin 45 g/L, creatinine 79.5615 µmol/L, glucose 4.9950 mmol/L, CRP 0.1 mg/dL. Then

xb = −19.9067 − 0.0336(45) + 0.0095(79.5615) + 0.1953(4.9950) + 0.0954·ln(0.1)
     − 0.0120(30) + 0.0268(90) + 0.3306(13) + 0.0019(70) + 0.0554(6.0) + 0.0804(50)
   = −9.0718
m        = 1 − exp( −exp(xb) · (exp(120·0.0076927) − 1) / 0.0076927 )
PhenoAge = 141.50225 + ln(−0.00553·ln(1 − m)) / 0.090165  =  41.85 years

Put the unconverted numbers into the same weights and xb is 8.36, m is 1, and there is no age.

Horvath output transform. A raw linear score of −0.5 becomes 21·exp(−0.5) − 1 = 11.74 years; a score of 1.2 becomes 21·1.2 + 20 = 45.2 years.

GrimAge2 Cox → years, with the constants of pyaging’s GrimAge2.postprocess (FALCONAge ships GrimAge2 as a licensed scaffold, so these are not its numbers to publish). A Cox linear predictor of 16.0 becomes ((16.0 − 15.370829484122)/1.09534876966487) × 9.05974444998421 + 66.0943807965085 = 71.30 years.

tAge chronological, mouse. The chronological clocks predict age divided by the species’ maximum recorded lifespan, and the mortality clocks predict log10 of the expected mortality rate (Tyshkovskiy et al., Nature 2026); the mouse’s maximum recorded lifespan is 4 years in AnAge. A model output of 0.35 (fraction of maximum lifespan) becomes 0.35 × 48 = 16.8 months. The same 0.35 from a mortality clock stays as log10(HR) = 0.35, i.e. HR ≈ 2.24 - never 16.8 anything.

Checked against the sources (2026-09-25). 5.1 against Levine et al. 2018, Table 1, Supplementary Table S1 and Supplement 1, and BioAge’s phenoage_calc.R; 5.2 and the GrimAge2 constants against pyaging at commit e7267e9; 5.3 against src/biological_age_correction.py and src/lifespan_prediction_with_error_propagation.py in Meyer-DH/AgingClock; 5.4 against Zhang et al. 2019 (the 2.04-year RMSE and the absent mortality association in both Lothian cohorts) and their pred.R; the Horvath transform against dnaMethyAge’s HorvathS2013_transform; the tAge scales against Tyshkovskiy et al. 2026 and the AnAge entry for Mus musculus.
