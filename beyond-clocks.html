Beyond the clock score

Reading an aging methylome without predicting an age

Every clock in the catalogue models the mean beta at a set of sites and returns one number. That is not the only thing a methylation matrix can be asked, and on the field’s own evidence it may not be the most informative thing.

Tong et al. [65] quantified how much of a clock is entropy rather than biology: roughly 66 to 75 per cent of Horvath2013’s accuracy against chronological age is reproducible by a purely stochastic model of methylation change, about 90 per cent for Zhang, and about 63 per cent for PhenoAge. The gradient is the interesting part. The more accurately a clock predicts chronological age, the more of it is drift.

This page covers the three things FALCONAge computes that are not clocks: the disorder readouts, repertoire structure, and where a clock’s coefficient mass sits. None of them returns an age, none is gated by scale_type, and none is comparable between datasets processed differently.

Disorder: entropy, drift and the barometer

falconage.disorder holds four functions, and the division between them is about what carries the number, which is the distinction most easily lost.

Function Returns Per what
fa.entropy how far the methylome sits from fully committed sample
fa.drift how far a sample sits from the cohort centroid sample
fa.noise_barometer summed per-site SD over age-variable sites group
fa.variable_sites which cytosines widen with age site

Entropy

Normalised Shannon entropy over the beta matrix:

\[ S = \frac{1}{N \log \tfrac{1}{2}} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \Big[ \beta_i \log \beta_i + (1-\beta_i) \log (1-\beta_i) \Big] \]

Bounded 0 to 1. A site at beta 0 or 1 is decided and contributes nothing; a site at 0.5 is maximally uncertain and contributes its full share. So \(S = 0\) is a methylome where every site is committed and \(S = 1\) is one where none of them is.

import falconage as fa

data = fa.read("betas.csv")
ent = fa.entropy(data)        # entropy, n_sites

n_sites is not decoration. Entropy is a mean over sites, so a mean over different sites is not a comparison. The default restricts to sites covered in every sample for exactly that reason; set complete_sites=False to use each sample’s own coverage and then read n_sites before comparing any two rows.

On the test corpus this lands at 0.695 with an SD of 0.007 across 825,177 complete sites, which is what a clean bimodal EPIC distribution looks like once it is summarised to one number.

Drift

The barometer is a property of a group, which leaves nothing to regress against an outcome. fa.drift is the per-sample analogue: the distance from the cohort centroid, computed leave-one-out so that a sample is never scored against a mean it helped compute.

dri = fa.drift(data)                          # drift from this cohort
dri = fa.drift(data, reference=young_mean)    # drift from a young reference

The leave-one-out default matters more than it looks. Scoring a sample against a centroid that includes it shrinks its own distance, and in a small cohort that shrinkage is large enough to invert a group difference.

Which sites actually widen

The barometer of Mei et al. [70] is defined over “cytosines whose variance rises with age”, and that clause is the whole statistic. Summed over every site instead, the sum is dominated by sites that are merely variable, most of which have nothing to do with age.

fa.variable_sites is that selection step, split out so it can be inspected rather than assumed. The test is Brown-Forsythe, which is Levene’s test using the median, across age bins. Bartlett’s test is the more common choice in this literature and is the wrong one here: it assumes normality within each group, and beta values near 0 or 1 are visibly not normal, so Bartlett reports variance differences that are distributional artefacts.

A significant result says the variances differ between bins. It does not say variance rises, and a site whose variance falls is just as significant, so direction carries the sign of the trend and rising is the conjunction the barometer wants.

ImportantWhat this looks like on a cohort that is too small

Run on the test corpus, sites_rising under Benjamini-Hochberg is zero. Twenty-seven samples in three bins of nine, against roughly 820,000 tests, has almost no power once the correction is applied.

That is the correct answer, not a gap in the implementation. A barometer that means anything needs a cohort in the hundreds. fa.noise_barometer accepts an explicit sites= list so a selection made elsewhere can be reused, which is also the only way the statistic transfers between cohorts.

Repertoire structure

falconage.immune addresses a covariate no published blood clock carries.

Deconvolution answers how many cells of each type. It says nothing about how many distinct clones those cells represent, and the two are different measurements: two donors with an identical CD8 memory fraction can differ by orders of magnitude in clone count.

Why clone count should move a clock at all

Take one cell type holding fraction \(f\) of the sample, split into clones of relative size \(w_k\) with \(\sum_k w_k = 1\). Give clone \(k\) a somatic offset \(\delta_k\) from its lineage mean at some site, with mean zero and variance \(\sigma^2\). The compartment’s bulk beta is the size-weighted mean, so

\[ \mathrm{Var}\Big[\sum_k w_k \delta_k\Big] = \sigma^2 \sum_k w_k^2 = \frac{\sigma^2}{N_{\text{eff}}}, \qquad N_{\text{eff}} = \Big(\sum_k w_k^2\Big)^{-1} \]

and \(\sum_k w_k^2\) is exactly the Simpson index. The variance a clonal compartment contributes to bulk methylation is inversely proportional to its effective number of clones, and clone count enters through that quantity and no other. Three equal clones carry one cell’s somatic state at full weight; ten thousand clones average it to nothing.

This is why the effect is invisible to composition adjustment: \(f\) can be identical between two donors while \(N_{\text{eff}}\) differs thousand-fold, and every published blood clock sees only \(f\).

The myeloid version of the question is settled. Nachun et al. [71] measured clonal hematopoiesis against six clocks and found 1.31 years of acceleration on GrimAge to 3.08 on extrinsic EEAA. The lymphoid analogue has not been asked.

Measuring it

div = fa.repertoire_diversity(clones, sample_col="sample_id", count_col="count")
data.obs = data.obs.join(div)

res = fa.score(data, clocks="compatible")
acc = fa.acceleration(res, adjust=["clonality", "cd8mem_fraction"])

fa.acceleration already accepts arbitrary obs columns as covariates, so the incremental test is one call. The question is entirely whether clonality adds variance beyond cell fraction.

Depth is the trap. Richness and Shannon both rise with sequencing depth, so an unrarefied comparison between a deeply and a shallowly sequenced donor measures the library rather than the donor. Pass rarefy="min" to subsample every donor to a common depth by multivariate hypergeometric draw, which is what a shallower run would actually have produced; sampling with replacement inflates richness at low depth, in the same direction as the bias being corrected. clonality, simpson and effective_clones are far more stable and are the ones to trust when rarefaction is not possible.

Simulating it

The mechanism arm needs no paired cohort. fa.simulate_clonality generates bulk methylomes at fixed cell fractions and varying clone structure, so anything a clock does in response is clone structure alone.

sim = fa.simulate_clonality(
    reference, {"CD8mem": 0.3, "Neu": 0.7},
    clonal_types=["CD8mem"],
    clone_sizes=[fa.immune.zipf_clone_sizes(n, alpha=1.0)
                 for n in (2, 20, 200, 2000)],
    n_replicates=20)
res = fa.score(sim, clocks="compatible")

reference is sorted-cell mean beta, features by cell type. It is not bundled: every deconvolution model in the registry is catalogued as untraced, carrying real metadata but no coefficients, so the reference has to be supplied. FlowSorted.Blood.EPIC on Bioconductor is the usual source.

NoteWhat the simulation returns on the test corpus

Across 42 clocks scored on 48 simulated samples at identical cell fractions, the median log-log slope of score spread against effective clone count is -0.494, interquartile range -0.559 to -0.415. The derivation predicts -0.5 and was not fitted to the simulation.

Among the clocks reporting years, dnamphenoage moves 4.35 years between an effectively-two-clone compartment and a diverse one, and horvath2013 moves 1.54 years. Both fall below a year once the compartment holds a few hundred effective clones.

Cell-type fractions were identical for every one of those samples, so composition adjustment cannot remove any of it.

Where a clock’s weight sits

A clock is a weight vector over CpGs, and two questions about that vector need no samples at all.

Is this clock exposed to genotype? If a clock CpG is under strong meQTL control and the allele frequency at that variant differs between ancestral populations, a systematic difference in clock output between ancestry groups follows from genotype frequency alone, with no difference in aging. Cruz-Gonzalez et al. [75] established that the major clocks lose accuracy in admixed individuals; whether the size of that loss tracks each clock’s meQTL exposure is open, and half of that measurement is a property of the coefficients.

Is this clock regulatory? Patel et al. [76] report that most CpGs used by established clocks do not overlap known transcription factor binding sites. Reproducing that against any annotation class is the same computation with a different list.

mass = fa.coefficient_mass({"meqtl": godmc_cpgs, "enhancer": enhancer_cpgs})

Why mass and not count

Counting annotated sites answers the wrong question. A clock’s behaviour is driven by its heavy features: a 353-CpG clock with two enormous weights and 351 small ones behaves like a two-CpG clock. The statistic is therefore the share of \(\sum_j |w_j|\) on annotated sites, with the plain count returned beside it. When the two diverge, the annotated sites are the heavy ones, and that divergence is the finding.

Run on the registry with each clock’s own CpG set as the annotation, which needs no external file, horvath2013 shares 11.6 per cent of its sites with dnamphenoage but 19.6 per cent of its mass. weidner shares two of its three sites with dnamphenoage and 89 per cent of its mass. Two clocks that share their heaviest features will agree with each other for reasons that have nothing to do with either being right.

FALCONAge bundles no annotation list. meQTL catalogues and regulatory builds are large, versioned and separately licensed, and freezing one into a wheel would make it silently stale. GoDMC and the EPIGEN meQTL database are the usual sources for the first question, the Ensembl Regulatory Build or a Roadmap chromatin-state track for the second. .attrs["unmatched"] reports how many supplied ids matched nothing, which is the check that catches an EPIC v2 list with _TC21 suffixes meeting bare cg probe ids.

Reliability: trait, state and technical

A published ICC answers “how reproducible is this clock across a cohort”. Someone asking “has my number moved” needs a different quantity, and the gap between the two is why clock ICCs in the literature read as reassuring while replicate disagreement of up to nine years is also true [68].

fa.variance_components separates them, given repeated measurement:

\[ y_{ijk} = \mu + a_i + s_{ij} + \varepsilon_{ijk} \]

for person \(i\), occasion \(j\), replicate \(k\), fitted by Henderson’s method of moments. Two ICCs come back and the gap between them is the point:

icc
On raw scores. Comparable to what the literature publishes.
icc_age_adjusted
The same after chronological age is regressed out. A cohort spanning decades holds most of its between-person variance in the age term, so a raw ICC largely reports that the clock tracks age, which was never in question. The adjusted figure is between-person reliability among people of the same age, which is what any individual-level claim rests on.

A clock whose raw ICC is 0.98 and whose adjusted ICC is 0.4 is not a precise instrument. It is a cohort with a wide age range.

vc = fa.variance_components(res, subject_col="donor", occasion_col="visit")
vc.table                      # var_trait, var_state, var_tech, icc, icc_age_adjusted
vc.replicates_needed(0.9)     # per occasion, to reach ICC 0.9

replicates_needed returns inf where no number of replicates gets there, and that is the informative case rather than an error. Replicates average away the technical term only, so a clock whose day-to-day within-person variance already exceeds the budget cannot be rescued by running the array again. The honest answer is then a second draw, not a second scan.

Components are not clipped at zero. A negative one means the within-level spread exceeded the between-level spread, which is real for a small design or a clock measuring nothing in this cohort, and rounding it to zero disguises both.

Mosaic ages within a tissue

fa.models.single_cell.scage gives an age per cell [28]. A bulk clock already reports the mean, so the quantity worth having is the shape of the distribution underneath it: a tissue whose cells are uniformly middle-aged and one holding a mixture of young and very old cells have the same bulk methylation and, plausibly, very different biology.

The obstacle is that single-cell methylation coverage is sparse, so each per-cell age rests on few sites and carries large error. Observed spread is therefore always positive, and reporting it as heterogeneity is the mistake fa.mosaic exists to avoid.

The null is stated plainly: every cell in the group has the same true age, and all observed spread is estimation error. It is simulated by drawing each cell from a normal centred on the group mean with that cell’s own standard error, taken from the profile-likelihood width scage already returns.

ages = fa.models.single_cell.scage(cells, reference)
mos  = fa.mosaic(ages, group="tissue")

A small p_excess is evidence of genuine mosaicism. A large one says the data cannot distinguish a mosaic tissue from a uniform one measured badly, which at typical single-cell coverage is the usual answer and is worth reporting as such. n_at_grid_edge counts cells whose estimate landed on the boundary of the age grid, where the likelihood was truncated rather than maximised; those pile up and inflate the tails, so a group with many of them should have its skew and kurtosis ignored.

What none of this is

None of these functions is a clock, and none returns an age. They carry no coefficients, no scale_type gate applies, and their outputs move with normalisation, probe panel and detection-p filtering. The only defensible comparison is within one processed matrix.

That is a real constraint rather than a disclaimer. Entropy computed on noob-normalised betas and entropy computed on raw betas are different numbers for the same DNA, and neither is more correct than the other.
